Philippines rental marketplace — business process v1

Prepared 11 November 2026. This is a recommended working plan based on the online research in [the research brief](./rental-business-research.md). It defines the proposed business before software development. Prices, demand, and operating costs will be measured as the service grows.

**Business purpose**

Help renters find suitable, available homes near their chosen destination, and help property owners receive relevant inquiries. Each listing belongs to the property's actual location, regardless of where its owner or the renter lives.

**First release scope**

- Coverage: properties anywhere in the Philippines.
- Categories: bedspaces, rooms, apartments, condominiums, and houses for residential rent.
- Participants: renters, direct owners, authorized property managers, and appropriately licensed brokers, with the lister's role shown accurately.
- Main service: rental discovery, clear listing information, and owner-renter inquiries.
- Business income: optional owner-paid promotion once there is relevant renter traffic; recurring owner services can follow demonstrated demand.

Lots, commercial leases, property sales, payment collection for rent, and international expansion are later scope decisions. Keeping the first release focused makes the initial operating process easier to run and assess.

**The operating flow**

Owner submits → listing reviewed → approved property appears on the map → renter searches and contacts the lister → parties arrange a viewing and rental → owner updates availability.

1. **The lister submits a property.** They provide their role and contact details, permission to advertise, actual property location, recent photos, category, available date, rental conditions, and costs. They can save an incomplete submission before requesting publication.
2. **The business reviews the submission.** Check completeness, address and pin consistency, obvious duplicates, and the lister's identity and authority under the defined review procedure. Request corrections when necessary. Reject misleading or unauthorized listings. A review claim must state what was checked; identity, authority, condition, and availability are separate matters.
3. **The business publishes the approved listing.** The public listing shows the rental price, price basis, costs, conditions, lister's role, and availability confirmation date. Exact public location requires appropriate permission; an approximate pin must be labeled.
4. **The renter searches their chosen area.** They select a destination, budget, rental category, and essential conditions. They can compare properties and save suitable choices. Their current location does not limit the area they can search.
5. **The renter makes an inquiry.** They provide a target move-in date, number of occupants, intended lease length, budget, and essential requirements. The lister replies and the parties arrange a viewing. Personal information is shared through an appropriate lawful process.
6. **The parties arrange the rental.** The owner and renter handle their agreement and rental payments directly within the initial service scope. The platform records only outcomes it can establish; a contact click is not proof of a viewing or completed rental.
7. **The lister maintains availability.** They can mark a property rented, withdraw it, or confirm it remains available. The initial working policy is a weekly confirmation reminder and a pause after fourteen days without confirmation. A paused listing needs fresh confirmation before returning to search.
8. **The business handles problems.** Receive listing reports, investigate, request corrections, pause disputed information when appropriate, and record the resolution. A rented or withdrawn property leaves active rental search.

**Required pricing information**

Every listing states the amount, currency, and rental period, plus whether the amount is per bed, per person, per room, or for the entire unit. Require advance rent, security deposit, known mandatory dues, utility arrangements, minimum lease, and the known amount due before moving in. Missing information should be labeled clearly. Placeholder prices that misrepresent the offer are rejected.

Useful Philippine rental details include individual or shared meters, internet, pets, parking, visitors, curfew, furnishings, and nearby landmarks or transport. Owner-supplied statements about commute times and flooding history must be identified appropriately.

**Revenue process**

Basic renter search and standard listings begin free. Once the service can show relevant local views and inquiry activity, offer optional, clearly labeled promotion for an approved listing. Before payment, state the placement, duration, price, reporting, and refund terms. Promotion follows the renter's location and search requirements and uses the same listing review standards as standard placement.

The owner pays the business for the stated promotion service. The renter pays rent and agreed rental charges to the owner. Promotion does not guarantee inquiries or a tenant. Set the first promotion price using actual audience and delivery costs; the earlier PHP 199 example remains a possible test price rather than an approved price or income forecast.

Monthly services for owners with several units are a later offer, supported by recurring value such as vacancy management or useful inquiry handling. Review the legal classification and operating requirements described in the research brief before beginning paid operations.

**How people find the service**

An owner publishes a complete listing and shares its link through existing channels, including Facebook groups where permitted. Renters opening the link can inspect the property and browse other nearby options. Promotion effort concentrates in areas with enough active listings to serve a useful search. Public coverage grows as participating owners publish properties.

**Responsibilities**

- Lister: provide truthful information, establish authority to list, reply to inquiries, arrange viewings, and update availability.
- Renter: provide relevant requirements, communicate about viewings, and report misleading information.
- Business operator: review submissions, maintain the listing rules, manage reports, support participants, handle its own promotion sales, and measure costs and outcomes.

**What to measure after launch**

Track active and recently confirmed listings by area, searches returning no relevant results, contact attempts, observable responses, reported viewing and rental outcomes, promotion purchases and repeat purchases, acquisition spending, and staff time. Keep observed facts separate from user-reported outcomes. Assess earnings after delivery and operating costs.

**Work sequence**

1. Business process: this working document defines the first-release scope, responsibilities, listing lifecycle, and revenue approach.
2. Screen plan: translate the process into map/search, property details, saved properties, listing submission, owner listing management, inquiry handling, and admin review/report screens. Optional paid promotion follows the revenue conditions above.
3. First implementation: build the agreed scope from the screen plan.
4. Initial launch: measure actual participation and activity, then improve the service and introduce paid offers when justified.
